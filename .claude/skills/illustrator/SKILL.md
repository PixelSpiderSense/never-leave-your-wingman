# Illustrator

Create clear, original visual stories. Start from the user's message, audience, and output format. For business workflows, use sanitized example data unless the user supplies approved content.

## Visual direction

Use the supplied Starplayer, Stay With Me, Mahaputra, and Hoshiko Satsuki fonts as style references only: playful weight, casual handwritten rhythm, restrained display flourishes, and energetic diagonals. Do not copy their glyph outlines, embed their font files, or depend on them in output. Baoyu and Ian references are moodboards for hand-drawn PPT composition, not templates to reproduce.

Favor warm paper backgrounds, charcoal ink, a small accent palette, uneven drawn lines, clear arrows, and generous whitespace. Preserve legibility and the user's brand where provided. Use `assets/FourCueInk-Regular.ttf` for short display text when the output tool supports it; use a widely available readable font for body text, dense labels, and scripts outside its character coverage. Provide an editable fallback when the font cannot be embedded.

## Structured diagrams

For workflow, architecture, sequence, or lifecycle diagrams, first record the nodes, directed connections, branch conditions, labels, and sources for factual claims in a small JSON specification or table. Use that structure to preserve meaning while applying the hand-drawn visual direction above.

Choose SVG, editable PowerPoint shapes, or self-contained HTML according to the requested deliverable. Check arrow direction, branch meaning, label accuracy, and source grounding separately from visual polish. Keep output within the user's chosen folder and use third-party marks only after checking their terms for the intended use.

## Workflow

1. Identify the story, required facts, and target canvas or slide size. Choose raster illustration, SVG, editable PowerPoint elements, or self-contained HTML to suit the requested deliverable.
2. Make one representative sample and show it before producing a larger series. Incorporate the user's feedback into the remaining visuals.
3. Build visuals with a consistent hierarchy: title, focal illustration, flow, labels, and evidence or caveats where needed. Keep operational facts grounded in the supplied source.
4. Render and inspect the actual output at presentation size. Check text coverage, clipping, contrast, arrow direction, and factual labels. Deliver the editable source when one was requested.

The bundled font covers printable Basic Latin, en and em dashes, and the rupee symbol. Treat it as an original display asset, not as evidence that the four reference fonts are licensed for business use.
